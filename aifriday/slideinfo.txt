These 3 slides are about **Vocal Accessibility (VA)**—making voice-enabled systems usable and inclusive for everyone, including people with disabilities, diverse speech patterns, different languages, and various environmental conditions.

# 1. Accessibility is Multi-Dimensional; Vocal Accessibility Runs Across All of It

## Core Message

Accessibility is not limited to vision or hearing impairments. Vocal Accessibility acts as a connecting layer across all accessibility domains, ensuring voice-enabled experiences are inclusive, adaptive, and trustworthy.

***

## Types of Accessibility

### 1. Visual Accessibility

For people who are:

* Blind
* Low vision
* Color blind

Supporting technologies:

* Screen readers
* Magnification
* Alt text & Braille
* High contrast interfaces

**Voice Accessibility Contribution**

* Voice commands reduce dependence on visual interfaces.
* Spoken responses provide information without requiring sight.

***

### 2. Hearing Accessibility

For people who are:

* Deaf
* Hard of hearing

Supporting features:

* Captions and transcripts
* Sign Language
* Visual alerts
* Speech-to-text

**Voice Accessibility Contribution**

* Voice interactions must provide text alternatives.
* Every voice output should have visual equivalents.

***

### 3. Speech / Vocal Accessibility

For people with:

* Speech impairments
* Diverse speech patterns

Examples:

* Stuttering
* Dysarthria
* Accents
* Non-standard pronunciation

Requirements:

* Inclusive Voice UX
* Adaptive ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition)
* Voice interaction standards
* PAS 901:2025 compliance

***

### 4. Motor Accessibility

For people with:

* Limited mobility
* Dexterity challenges
* Motor impairments

Supporting features:

* Keyboard access
* Switch access
* Voice control
* Eye tracking

**Voice acts as an assistive input method** when using hands is difficult.

***

### 5. Cognitive Accessibility

For people with:

* Learning difficulties
* Memory challenges
* Cognitive disabilities

Supporting features:

* Plain language
* Content simplification
* Guided workflows
* Assistive AI

***

### 6. Neurodiversity Accessibility

For people with:

* Autism
* ADHD
* Sensory processing differences

Supporting features:

* Reduced distractions
* Customizable interfaces
* Sensory-friendly experiences

***

# 2. Every Voice. Every Context. Adopting VA Standards

## PAS 901:2025

Full Title:

> "Vocal Accessibility in System Design – Code of Practice"

Purpose:

Provides guidelines for designing voice-activated:

* Systems
* Products
* Services

Ensuring accessibility for users with diverse speech abilities and needs.

***

# The Five Principles of Vocal Accessibility

## 1. UNDERSTAND

### Principle

Do not assume perfect speech.

Real-world users may have:

* Speech impairments
* Strong accents
* Age-related speech changes
* Temporary speech challenges

### Key Concepts

#### Every Voice Counts

Systems should recognize diverse voices.

#### Diverse Abilities

Voice systems must work for everyone.

#### Natural Variations

Speech differences are normal.

***

## 2. SIMPLIFY

### Principle

Natural conversations, not commands.

Instead of forcing users to memorize commands:

Bad:

> "Press 3 and say ACCOUNT\_BALANCE\_REQUEST"

Better:

> "What would you like to do today?"

### Key Concepts

* Intent over syntax
* Cognitive ease
* Inclusive design

***

## 3. ADAPT

### Principle

Always provide alternatives.

Interaction modes should include:

* Voice
* Text
* Touch

### Why?

If one modality fails, another remains available.

Examples:

* Voice → Text fallback
* Text → Voice output
* Touch controls

***

## 4. ASSURE

### Principle

Provide clear feedback and recovery.

Users should always know:

* What happened
* What went wrong
* How to continue

### Features

#### Clear Confirmations

Example:

> "Your payment was successful."

#### Error Recovery

Example:

> "I didn't understand. Please try again or type your request."

#### Helpful Guidance

Provide next-step suggestions.

***

## 5. TRUST

### Principle

Build trust through transparency and privacy.

### Components

#### Privacy by Design

Privacy integrated from beginning.

#### Secure by Default

Secure configuration out of the box.

#### Transparency

Users know:

* What data is collected
* Why it is collected
* How it is used

***

## Overall Outcomes

Voice systems should be:

### Inclusive

Everyone can participate.

### Intelligent

System adapts to user needs.

### Human-Centered

Designed around people, not technology.

***

# 3. Who Gets Excluded?

## Central Message

Voice itself is not inaccessible.

### The Problem:

Voice-only experiences exclude many people.

### The Solution:

Design for every voice and every user.

***

# Groups Commonly Excluded

## 1. People with Speech Impairments

Examples:

* Dysarthria
* Stuttering
* Cerebral palsy
* ALS
* Parkinson's disease
* Other speech differences

Example failure:

User:

> "Call Mom"

System:

> "Sorry, I didn't understand."

### Problem

Traditional speech-recognition systems often fail to recognize atypical speech patterns.

***

## 2. People with Hearing Challenges

Examples:

* Deaf users
* Hard-of-hearing users

Problem:

Voice-only response:

> "Please confirm payment of $120"

No:

* Caption
* Text
* Visual confirmation

### Result

User cannot access the information.

***

## 3. Non-Native Speakers & Accent Diversity

Examples:

* Regional accents
* Dialects
* Multilingual users

Illustrative failure:

User:

> "Book a cab to Behala."

System:

> "Booking a cab to Bengaluru. Is that right?"

### Problem

Accent misrecognition creates errors and frustration.

***

## 4. People in Noisy Environments

Examples:

* Airports
* Train stations
* Markets
* Factories

Problem:

Background noise interferes with:

* ASR accuracy
* Command recognition

Example:

> Metro announcements overpower user speech.

***

## 5. People with Cognitive or Memory Challenges

Examples:

* Elderly users
* Learning disabilities
* Memory impairments

Problem:

Complex instructions:

> "Press 1, then 3, then say your account number."

Too many steps.

Result:

* User confusion
* Increased errors
* Abandonment

***

# Design Principles to Prevent Exclusion

## 1. Multimodal by Design

Combine:

* Voice
* Text
* Visuals
* Gestures
* Haptics

Never rely on a single channel.

***

## 2. Captions & Transcripts

Every voice interaction should provide:

* Captions
* Visual text
* Transcripts

***

## 3. Language Inclusion

Support:

* Multiple languages
* Regional accents
* Dialects
* Multilingual conversations

***

## 4. Adaptive & Contextual

System adapts based on:

* Environment
* User preferences
* User ability

Examples:

* Switch to text in noisy environments.
* Simplify responses for cognitive accessibility.

***

## 5. Inclusive by Default

Accessibility should not be an optional add-on.

It should be built into:

* Requirements
* Architecture
* Design
* Testing
* Deployment

***

# Key Takeaway From All 3 Slides

**Vocal Accessibility is the practice of designing voice-enabled systems so that everyone can use them, regardless of speech ability, hearing ability, vision, motor capability, cognitive differences, language, accent, or environmental conditions.**

The guiding framework is **PAS 901:2025**, which emphasizes:

1. **Understand** every voice.
2. **Simplify** interactions.
3. **Adapt** through multimodal alternatives.
4. **Assure** with feedback and recovery.
5. **Trust** through privacy and transparency.

The ultimate goal is to build **inclusive, intelligent, human-centered AI and conversational experiences** that work for everyone.
